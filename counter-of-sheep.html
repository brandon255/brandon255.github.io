1 · The journey

Exactly what happens, end to end

From the moment the pack arrives to the moment a kid in another country becomes a friend. This is the whole product, in order.

1
The pack arrives — and they know what's coming

Excitement first. The Counter of Sheep puzzle pack lands in their hands with its cast of characters on the box, and the kid already knows the promise that comes with it:

"I get to explore, visit, and understand different cultures in different countries — with other kids."
2
They build their buddy at home, on their map

Dovetail pieces slide together — no glue, no tools, the shape is the instruction. The pieces are theirs to move around the map table, rearrange, take apart, rebuild. The buddy goes together the same way the world does: by hand.

3
The buddy wakes up on the phone

The app opens — no download needed at school or home — and the buddy they just built is there, alive. They talk: about art, about creative stuff, about music. The buddy listens, remembers, and answers in character.

4
BEEP — a newsflash!

Once a month, new characters ship — and the app announces them like breaking news. Every newsflash carries a "Did you know?" from history that makes a kid want to run and tell somebody.

See the newsflash below ↓
5
They look at the world map — who's out there?

The map lights up with chapter cities. "CreativeMornings… what's this all about?" Their buddy explains: makers, in 170+ cities, holding pieces of their island. The hunt is on.

6
The hunt — out of the house, into the world

Kid and grown-up go together — a workshop, a studio, a chapter breakfast. They find the piece, scan it, and it wakes up and joins their island. The find gets posted; kids in other cities see it and go hunting.

7
Pen pals — friends in other countries

Through the app, kids start writing to kids who found pieces in Lagos, Berlin, Mexico City. The buddy translates everything live — every message in both kids' languages. Different countries, different cultures, one island being built by all of them.

8
And it keeps going

New characters monthly. New cities joining. The Twelve history buddies unlocking one story at a time. The island never stops growing — and neither does the kid.

2 · The build

A joint you can feel, a language every piece speaks

Tapered sliding dovetail — pieces slide together and tighten as they seat; the last millimeter ends in a satisfying lock. Self-fixturing: the joint aligns itself, a piece only goes together the correct way. Positive mechanical lock: geometry holds it, not glue — infinitely rebuildable. And a universal interface: one dovetail profile across the entire line, so any piece from any city clips to any other. FDM printing now; injection molding, vacuum forming, and laser cutting in-house as volume grows.

Mattel quoted ~$100,000 to start a comparable program. That's what capability costs when you rent it. This team owns it.
3 · The companion

Comfort first. The teaching rides along.

The buddy is a comfort and support guy — like having a friend who takes you on adventures — that happens to educate. The science backs the ordering: children's comfort objects measurably reduce distress in new situations and function like a trusted presence; shared play lowers parents' stress while improving child outcomes. The education isn't a lesson attached to a toy — it's a friendship the kid keeps.

🧸A comfort object, built by the kid

Classic research: attachments to comfort objects are normal and healthy, and help children cope in novel situations.

Passman 1987, J. Consulting & Clinical Psychology; roots in Winnicott 1953.
💛Play that lowers parents' stress too

A randomized trial: programs promoting shared parent-child play improved child behavior partly by reducing parenting stress.

Weisleder et al. 2019, J. Pediatrics (RCT, N=362).
🗣It talks — and never phones home

The conversational engine is engineered in-house and runs local-first: children's data stays on the family device. COPPA-aware by construction.

FTC COPPA Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 312 (under-13 data protections).

7 · Pen pals

A friend on every continent

Kids write to kids. The all-engineered, local-first translator carries every message in both languages — nobody needs English to have a friend in Berlin, Lagos, or Mexico City. And the research says the ages matter: this is precisely the developmental window when contact across groups shapes who kids become.

🤝Contact builds openness — measured in our exact age group

A randomized trial with 983 students in grades 3–5: contact-based programs increased positive expectations about playing with diverse peers and reduced social exclusion.

Killen et al. 2024, Peace & Conflict (RCT, mean age 9.6).
⏰The window is now

Meta-analysis across nations: prejudice peaks in middle childhood (≈5–7) before declining — ages 6–11 is exactly when cross-group friendship changes trajectories.

Raabe & Beelmann 2011, Child Development; see also Beelmann & Heinemann 2014.

9 · The benefits

Not fluff — cited, area by area

Every claim below maps to a verified study or official source. Full citations in §14.

🔧Building → spatial & STEM skills

Randomized LEGO-construction trial (ages 7–9) improved spatial skills; block-play frequency tracks spatial skill in a nationally representative sample; preschool block play predicts later math achievement.

McDougal 2024 Dev. Science · Jirout & Newcombe 2015 Psych. Science · Wolfgang 2001 JRCE
🧩Puzzle play → thinking skills

Early puzzle play predicted stronger spatial transformation skill at 4½, controlling for background.

Levine et al. 2012, Developmental Psychology
✋Hands-on beats passive

Meta-analysis: concrete manipulatives outperform abstract instruction; interactive media beats passive viewing for young children (AAP).

Carbonneau 2013 J. Ed. Psych · AAP 2016 Media and Young Minds
📱AR engages and teaches

Meta-analysis of 64 studies: AR instruction outperformed non-AR on learning gains (d = .68).

Garzón & Acevedo 2019, Educational Research Review
🌳Outdoors → attention & mind

Park walks improved concentration (RCT); greener surroundings tracked better working memory in 2,600 kids; nearby nature buffered childhood stress.

Faber Taylor & Kuo 2009 · Dadvand 2015 PNAS · Wells & Evans 2003
🧠Mental health — kid

Completion confidence from building; a comfort object that reduces distress in new situations; real-world wins with a companion who listens.

Passman 1977/1987 Dev. Psych / JCCP
👨‍👩‍👧Mental health — parents

Shared parent-child play reduced parenting stress and improved child behavior in a randomized trial — the benefit nobody in toys leads with.

Weisleder et al. 2019, J. Pediatrics (RCT)
🌏Culture & empathy

Contact-based programs in grades 3–5 raised openness to diverse peers (RCT, N=983); intergroup training reduces prejudice in children.

Killen et al. 2024 · Beelmann & Heinemann 2014

Discipline note: we cite only what the evidence supports. Claims we deliberately do NOT make ("nature lowers cortisol," "active screens are safe," blanket LEGO-math effects) are listed in §14.
