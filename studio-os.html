What makes this different

  • It reads from the other lanes. Nothing here is typed twice.
  • Ordered by consequence, not by category or date created.
  • It answers "what did I promise that isn't done?" — the question that actually keeps you up.
  • When the day breaks, it re-plans instead of guilting you.

The rule the whole build follows

If a module needs you to maintain it, it's already dead. Capture is one gesture — say it, paste it, forward it. The system files. You never file.

Non-negotiable

Your students' names, faces, and artwork stay on your machine. They are never sent to anyone's cloud model. Every other tool in this category ships everything off-device.

Candidly

Eight things nobody put on your list

Ranked by what I think each is actually worth to you.

1Impact data

Funders renew on numbers. You've run programs since 2019 and probably can't produce a clean count of students served, hours taught, or schools reached. That count is the difference between $5,000 awards and six-figure ones — and it's the easiest thing here to start collecting.

2Your website

brendaarts.org is live but it's a headline and three sentences. A stranger can't see a single frame of your work or figure out how to hire you. It's the first thing anyone checks after meeting you.

3Speaking as a revenue line

Treefort, Summer Games Fest, Nike HQ, OMSI, CreativeMornings. You're clearly good at it and there's no pipeline, no rate card, no follow-up. That's money you're already qualified for.

4Curriculum as a product

You have years of tested lesson material. Museums and districts buy curriculum. One table serves one room — a licensed curriculum serves a state.

5Contracts and a rate card

You're hiring at $35/hr and working with minors. Contractor agreements, W-9s, release forms, work-for-hire language, and a written rate card. All cheap now, all expensive later.

6Render and asset infrastructure

The largest recoverable time sink for a solo animator: re-finding assets, re-rendering, versions that drift. Unglamorous, immediately felt.

7Archive discipline

Samantha is documenting Aug 21–22. That footage is grant reporting, pitch material, and two years of content — if someone files it properly the first time.

8Festival strategy

You've been in BlackStar. There's a whole circuit that leads to the exact buyers you want, and it runs entirely on deadlines nobody is tracking.

How we'd do it

The exhibition comes first. Everything else waits.

There are 18 days until Counter of Sheep opens. Building a four-module system during the worst week of your year would be the wrong kind of help. So we build the one thing that gets you through August, and it quietly collects the data the rest needs anyway.

Honest about status

Some of this already runs — invoicing, contacts, the daily planner, the local AI. Some is adapted from work we've shipped. Some is genuinely new. We'll tell you which is which every time, and we won't demo anything as done that isn't.

You approve everything

Nothing auto-posts, auto-sends, or auto-submits. The system drafts and tracks; you decide. Reaching out to the internet is a thing it asks permission for, every time.

It runs on your machine

Student data, releases, contacts, and finances stay local. That's not a preference — with minors in your files it's the only defensible way to build this.
